How to read it

One streak is a bundle of orders, not one parcel: particles are spawned in proportion to how much each corridor shipped that month, and coloured with that corridor's own late rate. So the density is the volume and the colour mix is the reliability.

The dots are not depots. Each one sits at the median location of that state's orders and stands for the state as a whole. Real parcels leave from many different points across it, so a corridor is a direction of travel rather than a road.

What the animation shows that a chart does not

One origin, one country. Press play and the first thing that appears is the shape of the business: almost everything leaves São Paulo. The spokes reach every state, but they are wildly different lengths, and the return traffic is thin. This is a marketplace with a hub, not a network.

Volume grows, and the shape does not. Through 2017 the streaks thicken month after month. What does not happen is any change in structure: no second origin appears, no corridor is rerouted. Whatever Olist did as it scaled, it did not change where parcels start.

Red is not where distance is. Shade by expected date and the map goes dark towards the Amazon: the North gets the longest dates in the country. Now watch the streaks. The long hauls north run blue for most of the window, because a 36-day date is easy to keep. The red concentrates on the short dense bundle into Rio de Janeiro and on the medium hauls into the Northeast, which is the same finding the distance chart makes and the animation makes it visible in a second.

February and March 2018 turn the whole map red at once. Not one corridor, not one region: every spoke changes colour in the same two months and then changes back. Nothing about what Olist was shipping changed in that window, which is why the report puts the question to the carrier rather than to the sellers.

August 2018 is the one that should worry a CEO. Scrub to the end. The streaks are the fastest in the whole animation, and São Paulo, the shortest corridor in the country, is turning red. The network was at its best and the customer experience was getting worse, because the expected date had been cut from 27 days in June to 13 in August while the network took 13.1 for the slowest tenth.

A caveat, as everywhere on this project. August 2018 is cut short by data collection, so its bar in the timeline is short for a reason that has nothing to do with demand. Corridors are drawn between the median coordinates of each state's orders, so an arc is a direction of travel rather than a road. And a streak stands for a bundle of orders, not a single parcel.

Data. The same 6.8%-late, 96,203-order window as the main report: Olist's nine Kaggle files joined on order, customer, seller and zip prefix, purchase months January 2017 to August 2018.
